# Stage 5 — Reflect

> Project Stage: 05  
> Purpose: Pause after completing the project and record how your understanding changed through the experience.

---

# Stage Purpose

The project ends here.

Your learning does not.

Until now you have:

```text
Started

↓

Executed

↓

Modified

↓

Understood
```

Now you are invited to ask one final question.

Not:

> **"Did I finish the project?"**

Instead:

> **"How has my understanding changed while completing this project?"**

The purpose of this stage is **not** to evaluate you.

It is to preserve your own learning journey.

---

# Why Reflection Matters

Programming projects usually end when the code works.

However, experienced engineers know that the most valuable part of a project is often not the final code.

It is the change in understanding that occurred while building it.

Examples include:

- discovering a better way to organize code,
- realizing an incorrect assumption,
- understanding why a structure exists,
- noticing a relationship that was invisible at the beginning,
- recognizing a question that still has no answer.

These changes are easy to forget.

Reflection preserves them.

---

# Reflection Is Different From Documentation

Reflection is **not** a project report.

Reflection is **not** a technical specification.

Reflection is **not** a GitHub Pull Request description.

Those documents answer:

> What did I do?

Reflection answers:

> **How did my understanding change while doing it?**

---

# Before You Begin

Do **not** try to summarize the entire project.

Instead, think about moments such as:

- something finally made sense,
- something became confusing,
- something surprised you,
- something changed your opinion,
- something made you curious,
- something you still cannot explain.

Those moments are usually more valuable than a complete project summary.

---

# Reflection Question

Every project provides one Reflection question.

Replace the placeholder below with the project-specific question.

> **How has your understanding changed since you first started this project?**

This question should encourage genuine reflection.

It should never suggest a "correct" answer.

---

# You May Write About

If you are unsure where to begin,
consider one or more of the following.

### What did you expect before starting?

Examples:

- I thought...
- I assumed...
- I believed...
- I expected...

---

### What actually happened?

Describe the experience.

Examples:

- I noticed...
- I discovered...
- I struggled with...
- I was surprised that...

---

### What became clearer?

Describe one important realization.

Examples:

- I finally understood...
- I now see why...
- I realized that...
- I did not notice before...

---

### What is still difficult?

Learning is rarely complete.

It is perfectly acceptable to write:

- I still don't understand...
- I want to explore...
- I need another example...
- I have a new question...

Sometimes the most valuable Reflection is recognizing what remains unresolved.

---

### What would you do differently next time?

Reflection is also preparation.

Examples:

- I would test earlier.
- I would separate responsibilities sooner.
- I would simplify the design.
- I would compare more alternatives.
- I would ask different questions.

---

# Reflection Guidelines

Good Reflections are:

- honest,
- specific,
- personal,
- concise,
- connected to experience.

Good Reflections are **not** judged by length.

One meaningful paragraph is usually more valuable than several pages of repetition.

---

# Reflection Is Optional

Reflection is encouraged.

It is **not** required to complete the project.

You may continue learning even if you choose not to write a Reflection.

However,

projects completed with Reflection often become much easier to remember and build upon later.

---

# Important Rule

The project should never write the Reflection for you.

The project should never suggest your conclusion.

The project should never evaluate your answer.

Only you can describe how your understanding changed.

---

# What Happens Next?

After you submit your Reflection,

InnerMirror may provide:

- a summary,
- pacing guidance,
- a continuity signal,
- a next question.

These are intended to help you continue thinking.

They are **not** grades.

# Relationship Between PBL, GitHub, and InnerMirror

The three systems have different responsibilities.

```text
PBL

↓

Project Experience

↓

GitHub

↓

Implementation Record

↓

InnerMirror

↓

Reflection

```

PBL creates the experience.

GitHub records what you built.

---

# Reflection Principles

Every Reflection in the MVP follows the same rules.

- Reflection remains a normal InnerMirror Reflection.
- No project-specific Reflection type is created.
- Reflection belongs to the learner.
- Reflection is never automatically generated.
- Reflection should not be pre-filled.
- Reflection should not become a project report.
